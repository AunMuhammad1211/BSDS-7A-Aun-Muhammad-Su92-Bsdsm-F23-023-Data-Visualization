## Panel decisions

Panel 1 answers: *Is length of stay concentrated near short admissions or strongly skewed?* A histogram is appropriate because it shows the distribution of one quantitative variable. It follows Week 2's **position and length** rule: the horizontal position identifies the LOS interval and bar length, measured from a common zero baseline, encodes the number of patients. Equal-width bins also preserve fair comparisons between intervals. Panel 2 answers: *Do typical stays differ across wards?* A box plot compares median, spread, and outliers across categories compactly. It follows the Week 2 **position** rule because ward is identified by position along the categorical x-axis and LOS is read by position on one shared quantitative y-axis; colour is only a consistent fill, not the data channel. The supplied wide LOS table was reshaped with `melt()` into one observation per ward before Seaborn received it. Panel 3 answers: *Which clinically related measures have the strongest relationship?* A scatter plot preserves each patient as a mark and follows the Week 2 **position** rule: both measurements are encoded by aligned 2-D position on shared quantitative axes, the most accurate channel in the Week 2 ranking. It shows the selected diastolic–MAP relationship without implying causation. Panel 4 answers: *Which numeric measures form correlation clusters worth investigating?* A lower-triangle heatmap uses Week 2 **colour intensity/hue** as an overview channel, while annotations provide the exact signed Pearson correlations. The heatmap also uses **enclosure/proximity**: each cell encloses one variable pair and nearby cells reveal clusters. Because colour is less accurate than position, the labels and numbers are retained as redundant encodings rather than asking colour to carry the result alone.

## Parameters

The figure is a 2×2 layout with `figsize=(14, 10)` and `constrained_layout=True`; the exported PNG uses `dpi=300` and `bbox_inches="tight"`. Panel 1 uses a declared `bin_width=0.5` days, a common zero baseline, and white bin edges. Panel 2 uses canonical ward order (General Medicine, Cardiology, Day Surgery, ICU), `showfliers=False` only to keep the long-stay display readable, and a fixed colour; the long stays remain in the data and are disclosed below. Panel 3 uses marker size `s=10`, `alpha=0.25`, and no edge colour to reduce overplotting; these are display parameters, not hidden data filters. Panel 4 uses `cmap='RdBu_r'`, `vmin=-1`, `vmax=1`, `center=0`, `annot=True`, `fmt='.2f'`, a square layout, and an upper-triangle mask. These choices make positive and negative correlations comparable and prevent duplicated pairs from dominating the panel. The palette is the course palette; category identity is not left to colour alone where a direct label or position is available. Spines are hidden, titles state findings, and axes include units. Following Week 2's one-pop-out rule, no panel uses multiple competing highlights: the finding is carried by the stated position/length relationship, not decorative colour.

## Rows removed and ethics

Ward spelling was standardized from 12 labels to four canonical wards; no rows were removed. Mixed admission-date strings were parsed with `format='mixed'`, `dayfirst=True`, and `errors='coerce'`; no dates failed, so no rows were removed. Readings above 250 bpm were removed as device faults (`n=29`), and the eight zero-bpm readings were removed under the same impossible-heart-rate rule (`n=37` total). Mean heart rate moves from **78.8331** to **78.0469 bpm** after those 37 removals. The figure was also produced without the exclusion as the raw-data appendix export, [04_clinical_review_figure_appendix_raw.png](<D:/University/ML Lab/Week 03/figures/04_clinical_review_figure_appendix_raw.png>), so the effect of the cleaning rule is inspectable rather than hidden.

BMI values below 12 or above 60 kg/m² were removed as impossible or explicit error codes (`n=486`, including 84 zeros and 402 values of 999). Mean BMI moves from **66.1770** to **27.3463 kg/m²**. The 18 stays above 30 days were not removed: long admissions are possible, and removing them only because they make the distribution less tidy would be an edit rather than a rule. The final analytic dataset moves from **10,000** to **9,478 rows** after the 37 heart-rate and 486 BMI removals; date and ward validation remove none.

The symmetry test gives the same decision regardless of direction: I would remove these values if they weakened a conclusion too, because the rules are defined before inspecting the panel result and are based on impossible device/measurement values, not on whether they make a pattern stronger.
